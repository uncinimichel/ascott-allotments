# Choosing varieties that beat South Ealing clay

For almost every crop on the plot there is an RHS Award of Garden Merit (AGM) alternative to the variety already chosen, and the AGM list is the only independent quality check the research could verify. The gaps in it matter, though. **No overwintering onion, no borlotti, no rocket, mustard, claytonia, lamb's lettuce or land cress holds an AGM.** **Every AGM pea is rated H2 and none is listed as an autumn type.** **None of the well-known blight-resistant tomatoes is AGM either.** Where there is no AGM, the best evidence is Charles Dowding's no-dig trials, a few university and levy-board trials (Cornell for tomato blight, AHDB for leek rust), and then sellers. On this site the threats that matter are winter wet and rot in clay, slugs, leek rust, allium leaf miner, outdoor tomato blight and courgette mildew, more than cold, because London rarely drops below -5°C. In practice that means choosing for resistance and drainage tolerance before yield. The standout swaps are: Cristo or Germidour garlic; Radar or Shakespeare onions (both seller-backed only); Oarsman and Bandit leeks; De Monica or Aguadulce broad beans; Meteor peas; Tuscany courgette; Uchiki Kuri and Crown Prince squash; Orla and Casablanca potatoes; Crimson Crush and Losetto tomatoes; Florence and Christine strawberries; and Four Seasons lemon. The fleeced winter bed could also take Grenoble Red and Winter Density lettuce, claytonia, Green in Snow and Red Frills mustards, Medania and Giant Winter spinach, and Palla Rossa or Grumolo chicory. One timing problem: today is 20 October, so most winter-salad sowing windows have closed and this winter's extra salads will have to go in as module plugs or bought plants.

## How to read the tables: one verified list, many seller claims

AGM status was checked against the RHS list "RHS Recommended: Award of Garden Merit – Fruit, vegetables and herbs" ([RHS AGM list](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/pdfs/agm-lists/agm-fruit-and-vegetables.pdf)). Most of the research used the December 2024 edition. The winter-salad check, done on 20 October 2026, found the same URL now carries awards dated 2025, so a few "No" verdicts for other crops could predate a 2025 award. In the AGM column, the two-digit year and the H rating (H2 = 1 to 5°C; H3 = -5 to 1°C; H4 = -10 to -5°C; H5 = -15 to -10°C) come from that list. The one exception is chard and kale, whose AGMs were confirmed on RHS grow-guide pages rather than the PDF. **Text in italics marked *(seller)* is a claim made only by a seed company or nursery and not confirmed independently.** "Best for" tags are synthesis from the evidence, not a source's own label.

A few rules hold across the whole plot. On wet clay, autumn-planted alliums and legumes are more likely to rot than to freeze. The RHS advises against autumn onion sets on heavy soil that waterlogs and suggests starting sets and garlic cloves in modules under cover ([RHS onions](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/onions/grow-your-own); [RHS garlic](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/garlic/grow-your-own)). Planting shallowly into the no-dig compost layer is the practical middle course. **No variety of anything here has documented resistance to allium leaf miner, pigeons or chocolate spot**, so mesh, spacing and airflow do more than variety choice for those ([RHS leeks](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/leeks/grow-your-own); [Bayer Crop Science](https://cropscience.bayer.co.uk/threats/diseases/pea-and-bean-diseases/chocolate-spot-beans/)).

## Overwintering crops: alliums and legumes go in now, and rot is the enemy

### Garlic (chosen: Solent Wight)

Solent Wight is itself an AGM. Its three AGM siblings are all softnecks, which store up to a year and rarely bolt. Hardnecks taste stronger but keep only 4–6 months ([RHS garlic](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/garlic/grow-your-own)). Dowding advises softnecks for mild, frost-free winters and planting in October–November ([Dowding](https://charlesdowding.co.uk/blogs/homeacres/grow-garlic-an-easy-crop-with-no-dig-hard-or-softneck-tips-for-harvest)), which fits Ealing. The case for Carcassonne Wight is flavour and an earlier harvest, and it rests on its seller.

| Arno | Softneck the RHS recommends because it stores well | No | Softneck | Storage |
| Provence Wight | Autumn-planting softneck *(seller list; no per-variety data)* | No | Softneck | Autumn planting |

### Leeks

There are 13 AGM leeks, and rust is the deciding factor in London. **Rust resistance is mostly a seller claim, and the RHS itself contradicts itself on Oarsman.** Its grow guide calls Oarsman "resistant to rust", while its plant page says it "may be susceptible", so treat it as tolerant ([RHS grow guide](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/leeks/grow-your-own); [RHS plant page](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/217953/allium-porrum-oarsman/details)). The only independent trial data cover commercial F1s. AHDB-reported trials rated Antiope, Belton and Duration resistant (under 30% of plants infected) and Roxton and Harston highly susceptible, and warned there is "limited recent data" ([AHDB factsheet](https://projectblue.blob.core.windows.net/media/Default/Horticulture/Publications/Leek%20rust.pdf)). The RHS says the genetics of resistance are "not yet well understood" ([RHS Allium rust project](https://www.rhs.org.uk/science/plant-health-in-gardens/pathology/projects/allium-rust)). Mesh the leeks against allium leaf miner whichever variety you grow.

| Porbella | Long cropping season, thick white stems | Yes (2015, H5) | *More rust-resistant (seller)* | Long season |
| Toledo | Long shanks for use November–March (grower blog) | Yes (2002, H5) | H5 | Long winter harvest |
| Stamford | Early, bulks quickly and evenly | Yes (2015, H4) | Rust resistance reported only in an unconfirmed grower quote | Early harvest |
| Krypton | Harvest from December | Yes (2015, H4) | H4 | Midwinter |
| Below Zero F1 | *Stands long without bolting, rust resistant (seller)*; one grower still saw rust | No | *Rust (seller)* | Rust, as a gamble |
| Musselburgh | Scottish heritage open-pollinated type, very hardy, mild; seed can be saved | No | Rust claims conflict | Seed saving |

### Broad beans (chosen: Aquadulce Claudia)

Aguadulce shares Claudia's H5 rating, so it is the like-for-like swap. Dowding pairs De Monica with Aquadulce for November sowing ([Dowding forum](https://forum.charlesdowding.co.uk/community/topic/over-wintering-broad-beans/)). He also says October sowing is "too early", giving tall plants that frost and wind then damage. That points to an early-to-mid November sowing in Ealing. **Bayer states that no broad bean resists chocolate spot** ([Bayer](https://cropscience.bayer.co.uk/threats/diseases/pea-and-bean-diseases/chocolate-spot-beans/)). Autumn sowings on wet clay can rot, and one guide calls spring sowing more reliable on clay ([Garden Focused](https://gardenfocused.co.uk/vegetable/broad-bean-varieties.php)).

### Autumn peas (chosen: Douce Provence)

### Kale

The curly AGMs are H5, the hardiest of any leaf here. Planted after peas, kale goes in later and smaller than a March sowing, which favours the compact Starbor and Yurok. **No kale resists pigeons**, so net it from planting ([RHS kale](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/kale/grow-your-own)). One UK grower finds whitefly "irresistibly" drawn to cavolo nero and Red Russian less affected ([Tabula Rasa](https://tabularasa.org.uk/2022/01/01/kale-cavolo-nero/); [Tabula Rasa](https://tabularasa.org.uk/2017/10/13/kale-red-russian/)). That is anecdote, and the pest claims for Sutherland and the early Nero strain come only from their seller.

## The fleeced bed can take nine more crops, but this year they go in as plugs

Dowding's last outdoor salad sowing is 10 September. Plants set out by mid-October mostly sit still through midwinter and crop again from February–March ([Dowding sowing timeline](https://charlesdowding.co.uk/blogs/no-dig-resources/sowing-timeline); [Dowding Substack](https://charlesdowding549359.substack.com/p/healthy-food-for-the-dark-months)). Endive and chicory needed sowing in early July. For this winter, then, buy or transplant module-raised plugs. Claytonia, salad rocket, chervil and land cress were still listed as sowable in a recent Dowding post seen only as a search summary ([Dowding](https://courses.charlesdowding.co.uk/september-advice-for-vegetable-growing/)). Slugs on wet clay are the main winter threat. That counts against pak choi, which Dowding calls a slug magnet, and in favour of his slug-resistant Grenoble Red lettuce.

## Summer crops: blight, mildew and a short season set the rules

### Tomatoes for outdoor pots

**No tomato is immune to late blight, and every tomato AGM is H1c, judged mostly under glass**, so an AGM says nothing about blight outdoors. The best independent evidence is Cornell's US trial for Mountain Magic ([Cornell](https://www.vegetables.cornell.edu/pest-management/disease-factsheets/disease-resistant-vegetable-varieties/evaluation-of-late-blight-resistant-tomato-varieties/)). Crimson Crush is the most-trialled UK option, but a South-West London customer reported plants "utterly decimated by blight" in August 2024 ([Suttons](https://hub.suttons.co.uk/gardening-advice/tomatoes-that-beat-blight)). Dowding says it crops well "before it succumbs to late blight in September" ([Dowding](https://charlesdowding.co.uk/blogs/no-dig-resources/seeds-varieties)). Resistance buys time, and the time it buys is August. A seller claims Crimson Crush and Mountain Magic excelled at Wisley in 2021, but no RHS report confirms it ([Sow Seeds](https://www.sowseeds.co.uk/collections/blight-resistant-tomatoes)).

### Calendula

Hoverflies have short mouthparts and need open flowers with exposed discs, so **single, open-centred calendulas beat doubles**. This rests on general pollinator guidance; no hoverfly-count trial compares cultivars. Garden Organic names calendula as a hoverfly plant whose larvae eat aphids ([Garden Organic](https://www.gardenorganic.org.uk/expert-advice/monthly-organic-gardening-advice/your-organic-garden-in-may)). ### Poached egg plant

**Grow the straight species.** *Limnanthes douglasii* holds the AGM and is "highly attractive to beneficial insects including bees and hoverflies". It self-seeds freely, grows about 15cm, and can be sown in autumn for April–June flowers. Garden Organic recommends it near fruit. Named selections are effectively absent: Shoot lists 'Rosea' (pink, really a subspecies), but no UK seller was found offering it. A claim of 50–200 hoverflies a day per plant from "Staffordshire trials" is unverified ([GardenUK](https://gardenuk.co.uk/how-to/how-to-grow-poached-egg-plant-uk/)).

## Conclusion

The research shows two kinds of gap. The AGM is strong where the RHS has trialled recently, as with strawberries, leeks, courgettes and potatoes, and you can buy with confidence there. It is silent or misleading where the problem is overwintering or field disease. Every AGM pea is H2, tomato AGMs are judged under glass, there are no overwintering onion or borlotti awards, and Safari and Carcassonne Wight carry AGM labels they don't hold. In those crops, a seller's resistance claim is often the only evidence there is. On this plot the realistic response is to hedge: pair an AGM with a seller-backed resister (Oarsman with Below Zero, Crimson Crush with Losetto, Florence with Christine) rather than trusting either alone.

The second lesson is that management on clay beats genetics for most of the named threats. No variety resists chocolate spot, allium leaf miner, pigeons or blackleg, and leek rust resistance is partial at best. Module-raising alliums and legumes, mesh, mulch and November rather than October sowing will do more for the garlic, onions and beans than any swap. The one place variety clearly changes the outcome is ripening in a short, damp season, which is why Uchiki Kuri over butternut and early, blight-tolerant tomatoes are the decisions most worth getting right. Gaps remain: Which? Gardening trials were paywalled throughout, Garden Organic gave no variety picks, and UK availability of Mikado spinach, Glacier pak choi, Valentin lamb's lettuce and Meteor broad bean is unconfirmed.
